## Outcome

Added the pkgdown article `vignettes/articles/wayback-change.Rmd`, "Watching Forest Change with Esri Wayback and
Sentinel-2". It covers the new_graphiti stac-ortho-mosaics box, with the published Bulkley `co_ff04` floodplain
outlined. `data-raw/wayback_change.R` makes every network call and commits 8-bit display rasters and CSVs to
`vignettes/articles/wayback-change/`; a warm-cache rerun reproduces every file byte for byte. The article itself
makes no network call. Three things were learned by looking rather than reasoning:

- Over a polygon, `dft_wayback_pick()` must be restricted to each capture's best-cover releases. Recorded in
  `research/esri-wayback.md`, "Picking for a polygon".
- The per-band `rgb_domain()` stretch suits false colour but ruins true colour.
- JPEG plus a 0 nodata turns dark pixels into holes.

Code review, four rounds after one plan review, found eight more defects. Six were in round 1. The two in round 3
shared one mechanism: a sentence describing what the code meant to produce rather than what the page shows
(echoed display code, and a clipped outline labelled as the floodplain edge).

After the PR opened, a look at the published Bulkley change layer (`transition_vector.gpkg`) against the
composites showed that most of the floodplain change sat across the creek from the 1 km window. The window moved
south and grew to 1.2 km. A wrong turn followed: the first harvest outlines read the layer's `in_harvest` tag as
containment. It is `st_intersects` (floodplains' `fp_disturbance_tag()`), and review measured only 13.9 of the
55.6 tagged ha inside a 2017-2023 cutblock. The panels now draw the BC consolidated cutblocks themselves, which
date the window's clearing (2020) and the strips east of the floodplain (2023). A new section explains the change
layer and tabulates its tags beside measured containment.

## Measurement

- Wayback over the box: 182 releases searched (2015-01-21 to 2026-09-24) draw on 4 dated captures. Three are
  sub-metre: 2014-06-01, 2016-09-11 (81% of the box at most) and 2020-09-18 (all 54 releases from 2022-05-18
  on). That turned the issue's premise (change in 2018/2020/2022/2023) into a design: Wayback dates nothing
  after 2020, and Sentinel-2 dates the rest.
- Metadata reads: 16 min cold at zoom 16:19, 32-40 s warm at 16:18; the whole script runs in 47 s warm.
- The clearing in the window: Sentinel-2 median red 43 / 158 / 88 / 130.5 in 2018 / 2020 / 2022 / 2023. That is
  forest, then bare, then greening, which dates the cut to between June 2018 and July 2020.
- The 2023 composite is brighter across the box (93% of cells brighter than 2022; median red 0.038 against
  0.023-0.030 in the other years), so not all of its pale ground is new.
- A wrong turn: a crude test on the post's own composites could not place the change at all. 2019, 2023 and
  2025 brighten across the whole scene, and the 2022 offset made 2022 look 99% "new bright" until removed.
- Tree loss (Trees in 2017, another class in 2023) in the box in the published change layer: 71.3 ha. Patches
  tagged `in_harvest` hold 55.6 ha; 13.9 ha of the tree loss lies inside a cutblock started 2017-2023 (largest
  patch 6964: 7.1 of 25.2 ha). Cutblocks started 2017-2023 in the box: 9 blocks, 8 mapped by the province from
  satellite change detection; the clearing is an 8.5 ha 2020 block outside the floodplain.
- Committed artifacts 3.6 MB, then 4.7 MB with the 1.2 km window; body prose 1,083 words, then 1,457.
